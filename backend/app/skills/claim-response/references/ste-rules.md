# Writing rules, from the principles of Simplified Technical English (ASD-STE100)

STE was built for technical documentation, not correspondence; the skill applies its writing rules
without its controlled dictionary.

1. One statement per sentence, at most 20 words.
   - Not: "I understood from your message of 29 October that the kickoff would take place on 5 February at your office, which is what I prepared for."
   - But: "Your message of 29 Oct says 5 February at your office (E41233). I prepared for that date."
2. Active voice. Use the passive only when the actor is unknown.
   - Not: "A new date was decided." But: "Your message of 18 Nov names a new date (E41600)."
3. Simple present and simple past. No "-ing" constructions where a plain verb works.
   - Not: "We were planning on meeting." But: "We planned to meet."
4. One term for one thing, taken from the claim. If the claim says "deadline", write "deadline".
5. No noun clusters of more than three words.
   - Not: "kickoff date change confirmation message". But: "the message that confirms the new kickoff date".
6. Condition first: "If you shared the change in a call, please send me the note."
7. Dates in full with the time zone: Thu 29 Oct 2026, 13:40 CET.
8. Facts in bullets: each bullet has a date, a time, an origin and an entry number.
9. Questions are open: "Can you help me find where this was shared?"
10. The closing proposes to agree in writing.
